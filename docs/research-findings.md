# Phase 1 — Research Foundation

**Project:** El Molouk (الملوك للتصدير) — B2B Egyptian orange-fleshed sweet-potato exporter → Western Europe
**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Status:** Complete. This is the evidence base; every recommendation in `00`–`02` traces back to a finding here.

## 0. Method
Three parallel research streams were run against **real companies and reputable references** (not assumption): (A) competitive & design analysis of European importers and Egyptian exporters, (B) UX & trust-building patterns for B2B produce export, (C) SEO best practice (2025–2026) and colour psychology for premium agri-food. Sources are cited inline.

---

## 1. Strategic thesis (the one idea everything hangs on)

> **A premium Egyptian agricultural exporter built to meet European buyer expectations.**
>
> Proud of its Egyptian origin, presented with world-class quality, reliability, and professionalism — international without hiding its identity. Governing design principle: **the product is always the hero** — the UI supports the sweet potato; it never competes with it.

Two distinct "schools" exist online; the approved positioning takes the *credibility* of the exporter school and the *polish* of the European-brand school, with the product itself as the centrepiece:

| | **European importer/brand school** | **Egyptian exporter school** |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Nature's Pride, The Greenery, SanLucar, Bakker | GEO Exporting, Daltex, PEI, Il Mondo |
| Wins on | Trust & brand *feel* — emotive, editorial, sustainability-led, whitespace-heavy | Technical *credibility* — spec-dense, certification-heavy, conversion-focused |
| Weakness | Hides specs behind a login; little hard data public | Dated visuals, clip-art/emoji icons, low polish |

**El Molouk should look like the first and inform like the second** — and escape the "sea of produce-green" by drawing its palette from the sweet potato itself (terracotta, earth, cream, a whisper of gold for *الملوك/"Kings"*).

## 2. Market context

- **Egypt became Germany's #1 sweet-potato supplier in 2025** ([EastFruit](https://east-fruit.com/en/trending/egypt-emerges-as-germanys-leading-sweet-potato-supplier-in-2025/)). Germany is the largest EU fresh-produce import market → a powerful origin-credibility hook to lead the brand with.
- **Buyers:** importers, wholesalers, and retail-chain procurement in NL (Rotterdam = key re-export/distribution hub), DE, UK, FR.
- **EU preconditions to win a programme:** *consistent quality + year-round availability* + food-safety compliance ([CBI market entry](https://www.cbi.eu/market-information/fresh-fruit-vegetables/sweet-potatoes/market-entry)).

## 3. UX & conversion — what moves the needle (ranked)

1. **Certifications are a gate, not a badge.** EU buyers verify GLOBALG.A.P. + a GFSI food-safety scheme (BRCGS/IFS) *before* they'll negotiate. UK buyers often make BRCGS contractual; Germany prefers IFS/BRCGS. → Certifications go **above the fold** *and* on a dedicated Quality page with certificate scans, scheme numbers, validity dates.
2. **Two low-friction conversion paths, not one generic "Contact us":**
   - **Request-a-Quote (RFQ)** — 3–6 fields. Conversion peaks near 3 fields and drops with each added field; keep ≤5, single column, labels above. Add **only qualifying fields**: *destination port* + *volume (containers/season)* — these instantly separate a real importer from a tyre-kicker.
   - **Floating WhatsApp** on every page — the dominant B2B channel for MENA-origin trade; buyers expect near-immediate replies.
3. **Per-product spec sheet (on-page table + gated PDF)** — the strongest lead magnet in this niche; a light email gate turns a download into a lead.
4. **Concrete proof of scale** (own farms + packhouse, containers/year, hectares, years exporting, ports) beats adjectives like "premium quality." A **stat band** is the sector-standard device.

**Trust signals ranked:** (1) certifications → (2) traceability/supply-chain control → (3) own farms + packhouse → (4) capacity/volumes → (5) years exporting → (6) client/retailer references & case studies → (7) Incoterms + ports/logistics → (8) named people + real photos + physical address.

**Anti-patterns to avoid:** certifications buried/absent; generic value props ("world-class quality"); a single long (>5-field) contact form; no calibers/container/shelf-life data; auto-translated gibberish; dated/slow/poor-mobile design; stock photos instead of real farm/packhouse imagery; hidden company identity.

## 4. The hero product page — a procurement-ready spec kit

European importers hide specs; Egyptian exporters publish them. **We publish them, with polish.** The expected structure for sweet potato:

- **Varieties + sensory descriptor:** *Beauregard* (deep-orange flesh, pink/red skin — the EU workhorse), *Bellevue* (orange flesh, copper skin).
- **Caliber/weight grades (table):** S <150 g · M 150–300 g · L1 300–450 g · L2 450–600 g · XL 600–900 g · G 900–1500 g. Emphasise **size uniformity** and **Class I** (EU minimum).
- **Packaging options:** open-top **6 kg** carton (retail), **8/10 kg** telescopic boxes, bulk **bins (~550–600 kg)**; note retail punnet/pre-pack + private-label capability.
- **Container loading:** cases & pallets per **20′ (10 pallets)** and **40′ reefer (20 pallets / ~1,200 cases)**, net weight/container.
- **Seasonal availability calendar (12-month strip):** Egyptian peak ≈ **Aug–Jan**, extended via curing + cold storage → framed as **year-round supply reliability**.
- **Post-harvest/cold-chain specs:** curing (heals skin, deepens sweetness — doubles as premium story), storage **12–14 °C / 85–90 % RH**, "never below 10 °C" chilling-injury warning, shelf life ~4–6 months, data-logger monitoring.
- **Comparison table:** Egypt vs USA vs Spain (flesh colour, shelf life, value) — a persuasive B2B device.
- **FAQ:** MOQ, Incoterms, lead times, private label, documentation, MRLs.

*Sources: [GEO sweet-potato page](https://geoexporting.com/home/products/fresh-vegetables/sweet-potato/) · [PEI Trade](https://peitrade.com/egyptian-sweet-potatoes-export/) · [Il Mondo](https://ilmondoexpo.com/product/sweet-potatoes-egypt/) · [IncoDocs – container loading](https://incodocs.com/blog/export-packages/)*

## 5. SEO findings (2025–2026)

**5.1 Core Web Vitals (table stakes competitors fail — hence an edge).** "Good" needs ≥75% (p75) of visits passing all three:

| Metric | Good | Poor |
|---|---|---|
| LCP | ≤ 2.5 s | > 4.0 s |
| INP | ≤ 200 ms | > 500 ms |
| CLS | ≤ 0.1 | > 0.25 |

Actions: WebP/AVIF hero images with explicit `width`/`height` (prevents CLS), eager-load the LCP hero + lazy-load below the fold, CDN + HTTPS, minimal render-blocking JS, clean URLs + sitemap + canonical + mobile-first.

**5.2 International SEO (the decision that matters most for an exporter).**
- ~75 % of B2B buyers prefer to buy in their own language, but **a half-translated, poorly-maintained multilingual site is worse than one excellent English site.** *Never publish raw machine translation* — Google can suppress rankings across all language versions.
- **URL structure: subdirectories on one domain** (`/en/`, `/de/`, `/nl/`, `/fr/`, `/es/`, `/it/`) — best for consolidating SEO authority; avoid ccTLDs for a small exporter. *(Project scope: these six locales, implemented via next-intl.)*
- **hreflang is high-risk** (~75 % of implementations break): every variant must reference every other **including itself**; one bad URL/ISO code makes Google ignore the whole cluster; always include **`x-default`** → English/global fallback.
- Distinguish **language** targeting from **country** targeting (currency, Incoterms, certs).

**5.3 Keyword architecture — high-intent long-tail over generic volume.** Pillars: *Product* (`orange fleshed sweet potato supplier`, `Egyptian sweet potato exporter`, `Beauregard sweet potato wholesale`), *Spec/logistics* (`sweet potato container load quantity`, `FCL sweet potato Egypt to Rotterdam`), *Trust/compliance* (`GLOBALG.A.P sweet potato Egypt`), *Seasonal/informational* (`Egyptian sweet potato season`, `orange vs white fleshed`).

**5.4 Product pages win when spec-dense** (≥1,000 words unique, spec-rich) — not 150 words of fluff. **~68 % of B2B buyers now use AI tools** (ChatGPT/Perplexity/AI Overviews) in vendor research; well-structured, schema-marked, table-and-FAQ pages are what these engines cite → **write for extractability**.

**5.5 E-E-A-T for food (YMYL-adjacent).** Reward: original farm/packhouse photography & video, named team + founder story, verifiable certifications, physical address + company registration, consistent NAP, trade-association membership. Penalise: vague unverifiable claims, anonymous authorship.

**5.6 Structured data (JSON-LD).**

| Schema | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Organization | ✅ sitewide | `sameAs`, `knowsAbout`, logo, address — feeds Knowledge Graph + AI |
| Product | ✅ product page | Entity clarity + AI extractability even without star ratings |
| BreadcrumbList | ✅ | Readable SERP path → higher CTR |
| LocalBusiness | ✅ (packhouse/HQ) | Reinforces physical existence |
| FAQPage | ⚠️ mark up, but **no rich result** for business sites since 2023 | Still helps AI extraction + on-page UX |
| Offer/price | ⚠️ omit price | B2B pricing is quote-based; only use truthful `availability` |

*Sources: [web.dev CWV thresholds](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds) · [Google – international](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites) · [Search Engine Land – international SEO](https://searchengineland.com/guide/international-seo) · [Backlinko – B2B SEO](https://backlinko.com/seo-for-b2b) · [Semrush – E-E-A-T](https://www.semrush.com/blog/eeat/) · [Digital Applied – schema 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/structured-data-seo-2026-rich-results-guide)*

## 6. Colour psychology & visual identity

**Premium formula:** one **dark anchor** + one **restrained warm accent** + a **soft neutral** background, with a **<5 % metallic whisper**, held to **2–3 core colours** with generous whitespace. Ratio ≈ **60 % neutral / 30 % dark / 10 % orange / <5 % gold**.

For an **orange-fleshed hero product**:
- **Desaturate the orange** toward **terracotta/burnt amber/marigold** — pure saturated orange + yellow + red is the fast-food/discount triad. Pair orange with **earthy green, not bright yellow**.
- **Anchor with warm near-black or deep forest green** — this dark ground is what separates "premium" from "generic packaging."
- **Cream/sand dominates** as background; whitespace is what reads as premium.
- **A whisper of muted gold** ties directly to *الملوك / "The Kings"* — a line, a monogram, foil; never fields of colour.
- **WCAG:** mid-tone orange **fails 4.5:1 on white** → never set body copy in orange; use it for large headings, buttons (on a darker shade), or as a fill behind dark text.

**Recommended palette — "Nile Harvest"** (safest premium default; full tokens & verified contrast in `01-brand-system.md`):

| Role | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor / body text | Warm charcoal-umber | `#2A2018` |
| Primary accent | Sweet-potato terracotta | `#C25B2C` |
| Secondary (freshness) | Olive grove green | `#56613C` |
| Background | Warm sand / ivory | `#F2E9DB` / `#FBF7EF` |
| Metallic whisper | Muted antique gold | `#B08D57` |

## 8. Google Ads landing & RFQ conversion (final research pass, 2026-07-21)

El Molouk is **primarily a Google Ads destination** for qualified RFQs, so these are the highest-leverage findings:

- **Dedicated paid landing page, not the homepage.** Dedicated LPs convert paid traffic ~2–5× better (≈5–12 % vs 1–2 %; one case: 28.9 % vs 7.2 %). → build a **stripped-nav `/rfq` route** fed by ads; keep the homepage full-nav for organic/brand.
- **Message match / ad-scent → Quality Score.** The LP `H1` should mirror the ad's keyword ("Egyptian Orange-Fleshed Sweet Potatoes — Bulk Export"), not "Welcome to…". Landing-Page Experience ≈ ⅓ of Quality Score; improving it can cut CPC ~22–37 %. Use tightly-themed ad groups + matching page variants (or DKI in the H1).
- **Above-the-fold anatomy:** keyword `H1` → one-line qualifier subhead (variety, grades, Incoterms/ports, volume, year-round) → **single** primary CTA "Request a Quote" (sticky) → trust strip with **concrete** proof (counts, cert badges — generic "trusted by thousands" now performs like no proof) → real product/packhouse/container photo. **Remove** nav, competing CTAs, footer link-farms, autoplay carousels.
- **RFQ form — field count is the biggest dial.** Sweet spot **3–5 fields** (~17–23 % conversion; collapses to ~11 % at 7, ~7 % at 10+). Keep only qualifiers: product/variety+grade (prefilled), **destination country/port**, **volume/containers**, **Incoterm** (dropdown), timeline, work email+company. Drop/soften: phone (−~5 %; replace with WhatsApp), job title, "how did you hear", required message. Multi-step for deeper qualification; inline validation on blur (+5–13 % completion). Value-led CTA ("Get My Export Quote").
- **WhatsApp + speed-to-lead.** Offer WhatsApp beside the form (ideally form submit pre-fills a WhatsApp thread). Replying within **5 minutes** ≈ 21× more likely to qualify; ~78 % of B2B buyers buy from the first responder → wire instant alerts.
- **Spec-sheet = hybrid gate.** Keep basic specs visible (technical buyers prefer ungated); gate the **detailed export spec-sheet / packing-list PDF** behind a short 3-field form and deliver it on-page immediately. Fire a **dedicated thank-you page** (response-time promise + one next step) as the Google Ads conversion event.

*Sources: [Google Ads – Quality Score](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167130) · [Foundry CRO – LP best practices 2026](https://foundrycro.com/blog/google-ads-landing-page-best-practices-2026/) · [Digital Applied – LP study](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-study-2000-pages-tested-2026) · [Digital Applied – form benchmarks](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/form-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026-data-points) · [RedMoxy – RFQ forms](https://redmoxy.com/how-manufacturers-can-improve-conversions-on-their-request-a-quote-form-rfq/) · [Amplemarket – speed-to-lead](https://www.amplemarket.com/blog/how-to-win-deals-faster-speed-to-lead-statistics-you-need-to-know). ## 9. Premium produce photography & art direction (final research pass, 2026-07-21)

The brand principle **"the product is always the hero"** is executed primarily through photography (~80 % of the visual identity).

- **Signature shot — cut-to-reveal.** A whole sweet potato is a brown lump; the wow is the glowing **orange cross-section** against earthy skin — *this is the brand's colour story.* Wipe the cut face and shoot within ~30 min (it oxidises/dulls). Make it the homepage hero, headline/CTA sitting in the **negative space**.
- **Shooting the product:** macro texture (touchable skin/grain/soil), three deliberate angles (45° appetite hero · top-down grading flat-lay · tight texture), **directional side light** (natural, warm — the workhorse; avoid flat frontal/flash), matte earthy props (weathered wood, linen, jute, terracotta, slate), a hard-limited palette so the orange flesh is the single saturated accent — **analogous with "Nile Harvest".**
- **Documentary supply-chain (trust):** real hands/dirt/tools, environmental portraits (not posed grins), one consistent warm natural-light grade across field → packhouse → product so the set reads *art-directed, not stock*. Pair **scale** (wide operation shot) with **care** (single tuber in hand) = capability + quality.
- **Placeholders (design phase → replace with real assets):** choose authentic, palette-matched, textured images; confirm a **commercial licence** for each. Avoid clichés (businessman-in-field-with-tablet, HDR blemish-free produce on white, costumed "farmers", globe/logistics clip-art) — buyers detect stock and it lowers trust. Sources: **Stocksy**, **RealAgStock**, Getty/iStock, Adobe/Shutterstock (filter hard by light/colour); Unsplash/Pexels as free fallback.
- **Web-trend confirmation:** minimalism + one bold hero + negative space; cinematic full-width imagery (obvious stock lowers trust); optional **scrollytelling** field-to-container process; persistent **certification/metrics trust strip near every CTA**; purposeful motion only (Framer Motion, minimal).
